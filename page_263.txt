As shown in figure 10.29, while walking on the pitched road on a hot
summer day, usually an illusion of a pool of still water is created in
front of the observer. When the observer reaches that place, the road
is found to be dry. This type of illusion is called a mirage.

Mirage occurs due to the total internal reflection of light. When the
surface of the earth gets heated up due to intense sunlight, there is a
formation of different layers of air having a different temperature that
decreases as we go up. This happens because the layer which is closer
to the hot surface becomes hotter than the above one. That is why
the refractive index of the upper layer is higher than that of the layer
just below it. Thehot layerbehaves like the rarer medium whereas the
comparatively colder layer behaves like the denser medium. Hence,
the reflected rays from tall objects in the areatravel down from the
cold layer (denser medium) tothe hotter layer (rarer medium). When
refraction of light occurs continuously at different air layers as it
travels from denser to rarer medium, at a certain layer, the angle of
incidence becomes greater than the critical angle for that layer.
