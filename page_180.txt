According to the laws of motion propounded by Aristotle, who was
born in Greece in 384 BC, heavier objects fall before lighter ones.
This law was disproved by Galileo's experiment in the seventeenth
century. Around the year 1590 BC, Galileo dropped two balls together
from the Leaning Tower of Pisa in Italy and found that both balls hit
the ground together. He concluded that all freely falling bodies fall
with the same acceleration due to gravity. This was later proved by
the feather- and coin experiment.
